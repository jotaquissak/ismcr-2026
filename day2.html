Abstract — VR and Robotic Rehabilitation: Results of a Systematic Review Following PRISMA Guidelines

In recent years, the growing elderly population and the global spread of infectious diseases have significantly increased the importance of rehabilitation utilizing Virtual Reality (VR) and robotics. Various studies have explored rehabilitation by integrating wearable robotic devices within VR environments. However, methodological heterogeneity and inconsistencies in defining "virtual reality" and "robotics" complicate clinical translation. This study details a systematic review methodology using the PRISMA 2020 framework to evaluate the effectiveness of combined immersive extended reality (XR) and robotic interventions. A structured search across six databases (PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, OVID, ProQuest, and CINAHL) for literature published between July 2015 and July 2025 yielded 3,874 records. After removing duplicates and conducting title/abstract screening, 39 articles underwent full-text review, with only 7 articles meeting the strict inclusion criteria of utilizing truly immersive XR and active robotic systems for affected clinical populations. Initial Risk of Bias assessment using ROBINS-I demonstrated that most included non-randomized studies carry serious or critical risk of bias, primarily due to confounding factors, small sample sizes, and a lack of control groups. These findings highlight a critical shortage of rigorous clinical proof-of-concept studies and underscore the necessity for standardized definitions and high-quality trials to support clinical implementation.

Abstract — Iterated Invariant EKF for 3D Landmark-Aided Inertial Navigation

Inertial navigation systems aided by three-dimensional landmark measurements constitute a fundamental problem in robotic perception and state estimation. Classical SO(3)-based Extended Kalman Filter (SO(3)-EKF) approaches provide practical solutions, but suffer from the false observability problem, in which the filter becomes overconfident in unobservable directions, leading to degraded estimation performance. The Invariant EKF (IEKF) addresses this limitation by reformulating the system dynamics as a group-affine system on a Lie group, although its measurement update does not fully satisfy certain state compatibility properties. More recently, the Iterated Invariant EKF (IterIEKF) was proposed to further improve the IEKF by ensuring, in the low-noise regime, that the estimated state remains on the observed state manifold while the uncertainty is confined to its tangent space. In this work, we formulate and apply the IterIEKF to landmark-based inertial 3D localization for the first time. Through numerical simulations, we show that the proposed approach outperforms the classical SO(3)-EKF, the Iterated SO(3)-EKF, and the IEKF in terms of both estimation accuracy and consistency.

Abstract — Perimeter Defense Game with Obstacles

The perimeter defense game is a type of reach-avoid game, where the goal of the attacker team is to reach the target area and the defender team aims to prevent it. In this paper, the problem of guarding a convex target area wherein the Defender is constrained to move along its perimeter is posed and solved. The mobile Attacker seeks to reach the perimeter of the convex target. The Attacker and the Defender have the same speed. The Defender can execute a point capture, which means it can intercept with a zero capture radius. In a game region, there is an obstacle outside the target area that the Attacker must avoid. The Attacker and Defender play a zero-sum differential game where the payoff/cost is the terminal distance between the Defender and the Attacker measured along the perimeter of the target. The Attacker wins if it reaches the perimeter of the target before the Defender can make an interception. The solution method first solves the problem without the obstacle; then, if the optimal path of the Attacker is blocked by the obstacle, the tree-like graph is constructed. The tree-like graph consists of the tangents between the obstacle and the Attacker, the arcs between the tangent points along the obstacle, and the tangents between the obstacle and the target. The new candidates for the breach point are the tangent points of the tangents between the obstacle and the target along the target. The path of the Attacker is chosen from the tree-like graph to maximize the payoff.

Abstract — Covert System Identification Under Timing Constraints: A Static Linear Model and Its Detectability Implications

This paper studies covert system identification in cyberphysical systems, where an attacker infers plant dynamics while attempting to remain stealthy. We introduce a lightweight static linear model in which the time to execute a setpoint change is proportional to the commanded speed difference. On a Siemens PROFINET testbed this gain captures the drive's ramp response accurately but not its terminal overshoot. That omitted transient becomes the attacker's main exposure: on real traces, the residual left by the linear masking procedure is detected by two anomaly monitors, Process-Aware Stealthy-Attack Detection (PASAD) and the CUSUM change detector. Across the cascade identification and staircase attack strategies defined in the paper, the stealth boundary relates to the plant's own settling transients, a structural limit for this class of models under the adopted detector-calibration regime. Comparison against dynamic models obtained by N4SID, trained at multiple excitation amplitudes, shows that the evaluated N4SID models do not improve stealth over the structure-informed ramp model. Since per-cycle computation is negligible for both procedures, the binding trade-off is not inference cost but whether the attacker can acquire and refresh a model without injecting detectable perturbations.

Abstract — EmbodiedAI: A Hardware Abstraction Layer for the Unitree G1 EDU

Programming the Unitree G1 EDU requires developers to integrate multiple software components, including network communication, RGB-D camera configuration, object detection, depth estimation, and locomotion and arm-control clients. This paper introduces EmbodiedAI, a platform-specific Python integration layer that encapsulates recurring initialization and subsystem-management procedures for an Intel RealSense D4xx camera, an Ultralytics YOLO detector, and the Unitree G1 EDU SDK. The evaluation comprises ten paired client-side implementations organized into basic, intermediate, and advanced tasks. Each task was implemented with and without the proposed framework. The evaluated static metrics include non-empty and non-comment Lines of Code (LOC), Selected Control-Flow Construct Count (CFCC), defined as the number of if, for, while, and try constructs, and the WITHOUT/WITH LOC ratio. Across all ten experiments, the framework reduced client-side LOC by an average of 51.6%. For the basic and intermediate experiments, the average LOC reduction was 60.8%, while CFCC was reduced by 62.7%, excluding Experiment 3, in which both implementations contained no selected control-flow constructs. The reductions became smaller in the advanced experiments, reaching 4.2% for LOC in the complete social state-machine example. The results indicate that the framework reduces recurring client-side integration code in the evaluated examples, while the benefit decreases when task-specific control logic becomes dominant. The evaluation is limited to static client-side metrics and does not establish improvements in development time, cognitive load, defect rates, maintainability, or real-time performance.

Abstract — ResNet+IMU Sensor Fusion for Frame-to-Frame Visual-Inertial Translation Estimation in UAVs

Visual-Inertial Odometry (VIO) is important for UAV navigation in GPS-denied environments, where camera and inertial measurement unit (IMU) measurements can provide short-term motion estimates under changing visual and motion conditions. This paper evaluates a lightweight ResNet+IMU fusion architecture for frame-to-frame 3D translation estimation using the EuRoC Micro Aerial Vehicle (MAV) dataset. The model combines visual features extracted from a pre-trained ResNet-50 backbone with summarized inertial measurements and adopts a multi-task formulation that jointly estimates relative translation and classifies motion magnitude. Two experimental configurations are evaluated: a baseline using mean IMU features and MSE regression loss, and an expanded configuration using mean and standard-deviation IMU features, visual data augmentation, Smooth L1 regression loss, and additional training samples. Under the reported validation protocol, the expanded configuration reduced the average translation MAE by 31.8% and achieved a frame-to-frame translation MAE of 4.8 mm on the V1 01 easy sequence. However, the same configuration increased the MSE by 436.7% on the MH 05 difficult sequence, revealing sporadic high-deviation failures despite improved average error. Since multiple factors differ between the two configurations, the results should be interpreted as a configuration-level comparison rather than an isolated ablation study. The findings highlight the need for outlier analysis, uncertainty discussion, and reliability-aware evaluation before considering real-world UAV deployment.

Abstract — Measurement-Driven Evaluation of Physics-Informed Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning

Reliable deployment of autonomous UAV swarms requires coordination policies capable of maintaining safety and performance under operating conditions not encountered during training. Although Physics-Informed Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) has recently shown promising generalization capabilities in distributed robotic coordination, its quantitative characterization in aerial swarm systems remains insufficiently explored from a measurement and reproducibility perspective. This paper presents a reproducible measurement-driven evaluation framework for decentralized UAV swarm coordination and applies it to IPPO, MADDPG, MAPPO, and Physics-Informed MARL (Ph-MARL) using the VMAS multi-agent physics simulator in the fixed altitude Reverse Transport scenario. Here, measurement-driven denotes a predeclared evaluation contract in which controllers are assessed using consistent metrics, thresholds, aggregation rules, uncertainty reporting, and out-of-distribution (OOD) perturbations before cross-controller comparisons are performed. The framework systematically evaluates policy robustness in a controlled physics-based simulation environment under sensor noise, observation delay, actuation disturbance, and combined stress using complementary measures of coordination quality, operational safety, control regularity, and generalization degradation. In Ph-MARL, the physics-informed component is provided by the port-Hamiltonian/IDA-PBC policy structure, which constrains the learned energy, interconnection, and damping terms according to the robot interaction graph; the present study does not model full UAV dynamics such as attitude, thrust, or aerodynamics. Results identify which measurements satisfy a common evaluation contract and which remain evaluator-specific, supporting fair comparison only for harmonized metrics while preserving within-controller OOD analysis for source-specific measures. By establishing a reproducible measurement protocol for evaluating generalization robustness in aerial swarm coordination, this work advances the quantitative validation of physics-informed reinforcement learning for deployment-oriented autonomous multi-UAV systems.

Abstract — A Reproducible 3D-Printed Robotic Fixture with Potentiometric Feedback and Vision-Based Readout for Handheld Multimeter Calibration

Handheld digital multimeters are designed mainly for human operation. Rotary selectors, model-dependent housings, manually routed test leads, and displays without machine-readable interfaces require repeated physical and visual interaction during calibration. This paper presents a non-invasive robotic fixture that combines parametric 3D-printed mechanics, a continuous-rotation servo, potentiometric feedback, computer vision, and a programmable reference calibrator. The modular PETG structure uses sliding supports, compliant alligator-style clamps, an adaptable selector grip, and a herringbone gear reduction. The reduction increases the available selector torque and expands the angular excursion measured by a multiturn 10-kΩ potentiometer. An STM32F1 device, from the STM32 family of 32-bit microcontrollers, reads the sensor through its ADC and controls selector positioning. New instruments are configured through a teach-and-repeat procedure, while positions, functions, calibration points, terminal information, and display regions are stored in editable JSON files. A specialized seven-segment OCR pipeline localizes and rectifies the display, compensates illumination, groups segment candidates, and validates readings by temporal consensus. The application coordinates the fixture and a Fluke 5520A calibrator, applies plausibility checks, and writes tab-separated data for spreadsheets or third-party calibration systems. Manual mounting, profile preparation, ROI confirmation, and lead changes remain operator tasks; the results are feasibility observations rather than statistical performance estimates. Functional tests were performed with Minipa ET-1002 and ET-3200 instruments and a Fluke 87V. Observed complete sequences required approximately 8 min for the clamp meter, 10 min for the Fluke 87V, and 11 min for an 18-range sequence including fixture placement. Controlled repeatability, OCR-accuracy, mechanical-endurance, and safety studies are planned to advance the prototype toward a robust laboratory solution.

Abstract — Experimental Evaluation of UAV-Based Multimodal Imaging System for Produced Water Detection in Offshore Environments

Offshore oil and gas operations require reliable sensing to detect low-contrast produced water plumes on the sea surface. Agreement between the offline and real-time results was evaluated separately for speed, torque, and phase-current magnitude, yielding MAPE values of 0.85%, 2.10%, and 8.59%, respectively. The remaining discrepancies are consistent with the different numerical settings, sampling paths, and time alignment used in the two environments. These results establish the implemented platform as an intermediate step for controller verification before connection to an external controller or a physical electric-vehicle powertrain.

Abstract — Measurement-Driven Self-Healing LR2021 Mesh: ETX–Q-Score Comparison and Near-Margin Airborne-Relay Evaluation

Abstract — Cascaded Model-Free Dynamic Compensation for Trajectory Tracking of Cooperative Mobile Robot Formations

This paper presents a Cascaded Model-Free intelligent Proportional–Integral–Derivative (iPID) control architecture (P-MF) for trajectory tracking in cooperative mobile robot formations operating under dynamic uncertainties. The proposed controller combines an outer-loop proportional kinematic controller with an inner-loop Model-Free iPID dynamic compensator, eliminating the need for an explicit robot dynamic model while preserving the conventional cascaded control structure. The inner controller estimates the unknown ultra-local dynamics online using only measured input–output data, enabling accurate velocity tracking despite parametric uncertainties and external disturbances. The proposed P-MF controller is evaluated on a triangular formation of differential-drive mobile robots and compared with both a conventional model-based proportional controller with inverse dynamic compensation (P-ID) and a Fully Model-Free task-space controller (MF). Simulation results demonstrate that the proposed cascaded architecture achieves the best overall performance in terms of trajectory tracking accuracy and control smoothness. The superior performance of the P-MF controller is attributed to performing Model-Free compensation directly on the robot velocity dynamics, which preserves the physical structure of the system and enables more accurate ultra-local estimation than applying Model-Free control directly to the coupled formation variables. These results demonstrate that Cascaded Model-Free compensation constitutes an effective and computationally efficient alternative to conventional model-based control for cooperative mobile robot formations operating under uncertain dynamic conditions.

Abstract — An Error-Dependent Nonlinear Sliding Mode Controller for Robust Trajectory Tracking of an Aerial Manipulator

Robust trajectory tracking of aerial manipulators remains a challenging control problem due to the strong nonlinear coupling between the UAV and the robotic manipulator, as well as modeling uncertainties and external disturbances. This paper proposes a Sliding Mode Control with a Nonlinear error-dependent sliding surface for robust trajectory tracking of aerial manipulators based on their coupled kinematic and dynamic models. Unlike conventional sliding mode controllers, the proposed approach introduces a nonlinear sliding surface exclusively within the discontinuous control component, allowing the switching action to be modulated according to the tracking error while preserving the nominal continuous dynamics. Consequently, the controller enhances transient performance and attenuates chattering effects without requiring additional disturbance observers, adaptive laws, or intelligent approximators. The closed-loop stability of the proposed controller is established through Lyapunov theory, guaranteeing the asymptotic convergence of the tracking errors. Simulation results under complex three-dimensional reference trajectories demonstrate that the proposed SMC-NL controller consistently outperforms the ID, PID, and conventional SMC controllers by achieving the lowest ISE and IAE values, corresponding to improvements of up to 35.3% and 46.5%, respectively, while generating physically feasible control signals suitable for aerial manipulation. These results demonstrate that the proposed strategy provides an effective solution for robust trajectory tracking of aerial manipulators operating under uncertain conditions.

Abstract — Impact of Velocity Observers on Friction Compensation and Energy Reconstruction in Motor-Side Sensed Haptic Interfaces

This paper demonstrates that the choice of state observer directly influences the energetic interpretation of dynamic compensation in differential haptic interfaces instrumented exclusively with motor-side sensing. Three causal velocity estimation strategies were evaluated experimentally: Finite Differences with Butterworth filtering, an Alpha-Beta-Gamma observer, and a Savitzky-Golay filter. To enable a systematic comparison, observer parameters were tuned through a multi-objective Pareto-based synthesis balancing bandwidth, phase delay, noise sensitivity, and low-velocity tracking. Equivalent friction models were then identified for each observer using constant-velocity bidirectional sweeps. Real-time friction compensation experiments further showed that standard signal-quality metrics do not predict energetic consistency. These results show that the velocity observer functions as an integral part of the energetic measurement chain rather than a pure signal-processing block, directly influencing the inferred dissipation dynamics and highlighting the necessity of energy consistent observer design for dynamic compensation.

Abstract — Robust Impedance Control Synthesis within a Generalized Output Regulation Framework for a 1-DOF Mechanical System

This paper addresses the synthesis of robust impedance controllers for a 1-DOF mechanical system, which is important for safe physical human-robot interaction and versatile performance in general robotics, within a generalized output regulation framework. Building upon recently established results, including prior work co-authored by some of the authors, that demonstrate an equivalence between compliant control and output regulation, we consider a broader formulation of the impedance control problem in which impedance realization and disturbance attenuation are treated simultaneously. By exploiting the regulator equations to reformulate the impedance tracking objective as an output regulation problem, a control structure is derived that guarantees exact realization of a prescribed impedance behavior under nominal conditions. To enhance robustness against unmeasured disturbances, an H∞ synthesis procedure is introduced based on a bounded-real formulation, yielding a convex LMI condition for the computation of feedback gains that minimize the disturbance-to-impedance error transfer. The proposed approach unifies impedance control, generalized output regulation, and robust control synthesis in a single framework, providing both compliant behavior and quantified disturbance rejection guarantees. Simulation and experimental results on a hydraulic 1-DOF actuation setup illustrate the effectiveness of the proposed controller under varying environmental stiffness and demonstrate improved interaction performance when compared with conventional compliant control strategies for stiffer environments.

Abstract — Characterization of Soft-Robot-Induced Variability in Ultrasonic Thickness Measurement

These findings indicate that soft robotic platforms can provide consistent ultrasonic inspection performance and represent a promising solution for non-destructive evaluation of difficult-to-access industrial assets.

Abstract — Quasi-Static Analysis and Experimental Validation of a Microspine Gripping System for a Quadruped Climbing Robot on Rough Concrete

The inspection of vertical concrete elements, such as walls, columns, façades, and bridge piers, often requires scaffolding, aerial work platforms, or manual access at height, which increases operating costs and exposes personnel to hazardous conditions. Climbing robots represent an alternative for accessing these types of surfaces; however, conventional adhesion mechanisms based on magnetism, vacuum, or gecko-inspired dry adhesives have limitations when confronted with the roughness, porosity, presence of dust, and geometric variability of concrete. This work presents the quasi-static analysis and qualitative experimental validation of a bioinspired microspine-based gripping system for a quadruped climbing robot intended for rough concrete surfaces. Numerical simulation results on the effects of the attack angle, the fraction of active microspines, and the total robot mass are also presented, together with a finite element structural analysis of the gripping module. Finally, experimental tests on rough concrete verify the mechanical anchoring principle and the system's ability to withstand external disturbances without significant loss of contact.
